First Try

See it. Get it.

A native macOS companion that watches your browser, remembers what you read, and answers when you draw a rectangle around something confusing — using your own recorded context, not a web search.

Download for macOS

Version 0.1.0 · macOS 26 or later · 749 KB · Apple Silicon

How it works

  1. 1
    Choose a window

    Click the menu-bar icon, pick your browser. First Try starts recording visible content quietly in the background.

  2. 2
    Browse normally

    Change tabs, scroll, navigate, come back. First Try follows along and builds a searchable local history of what you actually saw.

  3. 3
    Draw a rectangle

    Press ⌃⌥` and drag around anything confusing — a quiz question, an error, a term you've seen before but can't place.

  4. 4
    Get an answer

    A panel appears with the answer — drawn from relevant pages you browsed, not invented, not from the web.

🧠

Answers from your own context

The model sees what you saw. Relevant history is retrieved first; general model knowledge is clearly labeled as secondary.

🔒

Local-first by default

Runs on-device with a local model (Gemma 4, MLX). Nothing leaves your Mac unless you configure a remote endpoint.

⚡

No web search, ever

First Try never fetches URLs, calls search engines, or lets the model browse. Your evidence stays local; answers stay grounded.

🎯

Quiz questions, errors, concepts

Select a question and get the answer. Select an error and get the fix. Select a term and get the explanation — from pages you actually read.

📍

Lives in the menu bar

No Dock clutter. The icon shows recording status at a glance. The control popover opens on click; the floating answer panel appears at the selection.

🕒

24-hour rolling history

Evidence expires after 24 hours and never grows past 1 GB. No manual cleanup, no surprise disk usage.

Why a visual context engine?

Most AI tools answer from training data. First Try answers from your data — the pages you opened, the docs you scrolled, the tabs you kept coming back to. The difference matters: it doesn't hallucinate a memory of something you never read, and it can cite exactly where it found the answer.

The visual part is key. There's no browser extension, no tab-URL access, no paste-and-ask. You draw a rectangle on screen. That's the interface. Whatever is visible at selection time — a quiz, a terminal error, a diagram caption, a checkbox — becomes the question. The rest is automatic.

Opening it the first time

First Try is ad-hoc signed but not notarized. When macOS blocks it, right-click (or Control-click) First Try.app and choose Open, then confirm. You only need to do this once.

You can also run this in Terminal to remove the quarantine attribute: 

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "First Try.app"

First Try will ask for Screen Recording permission on first launch. Grant it in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen Recording, then relaunch. Open Settings (⌘,) to configure your model endpoint.
